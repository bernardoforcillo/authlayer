---
title: "Data protection (GDPR)"
description: "Which GDPR duty each authlayer call meets, the one place the library cannot help (rectification of what happened), and the long list that stays yours: lawful basis, notices, processors, breaches, backups."
---

No library makes a deployment compliant; most of the GDPR is about what your
organization decides and documents. What a library can do is make the
**technical** duties one call each, and refuse to be the thing in their way.
This page maps each duty to the call that meets it, and is honest about what
does not.

## Duty to call

| GDPR | Duty | authlayer |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 5(1)(c) | Data minimization | Hook events carry ids and a closed `Detail` vocabulary — never an email or a token. The audit log redacts secrets from `Request` and `Changes`, and can [truncate, hash or drop IPs](/audit/personal-data#ip-addresses-and-user-agents) and drop user agents. |
| Art. 5(1)(e) | Storage limitation | `audit.Topic.Retention` and `ApplyRetention`; a shorter `ClientDataRetention` for [IP and user agent](/audit/personal-data#a-shorter-retention-for-client-data); `PurgeExpired` for sessions, verifications and invites. |
| Art. 7 | Proof and withdrawal of consent | [`consent`](/privacy/consent): append-only records, `Accepted` before you act, `Withdraw` as easy as `Grant`. |
| Arts. 15, 20 | Access and portability | [`privacy.Exporter`](/privacy/access-and-erasure): one JSON bundle across auth, memberships, grants, consent, the audit trail and your own tables — no credential in it, no other person's identity. |
| Art. 16 | Rectification | The one personal field authlayer stores that a person controls is their email: `RequestEmailChange` and `VerifyEmail` move it, re-verified. See [below](#rectification). |
| Art. 17 | Erasure | `DeleteAccount` (removed) and `AnonymizeAccount` (scrubbed); `RemoveUserSweeper` for memberships; [`privacy.ErasureSweeper`](/privacy/access-and-erasure#erasure) for the audit log and consent; `scope.WithAnonymizer` and `WithPseudonymKey` for contributions that must outlive the person. |
| Art. 17 vs. an immutable log | Erase *from* the log | [Pseudonyms at write time, key destroyed on request](/audit/personal-data): the events and every seal stay; the link to the person goes. |
| Arts. 4(5), 25 | Pseudonymization, data protection by design | The audit pseudonyms above; `scope.WithPseudonymKey` for a stable per-container pseudonym; hashed passwords, tokens and API keys at rest. |
| Art. 32 | Security of processing | Refresh rotation with replay detection, step-up MFA, hashed secrets, a [tamper-evident audit log](/audit/integrity), database guards that refuse rewrites. |
| Art. 5(2) | Accountability | The audit log, `Filter.Member` for one person's whole trail, the consent history. |

## Rectification

Art. 16 is the right to have *inaccurate* data corrected. authlayer stores one
such field — the email address — and changes it with a re-verification
(`RequestEmailChange`, `VerifyEmail`). Passkey and trusted-device labels are
names the person chose; delete and re-register to rename. The `consent` store and
the audit log are records of **what happened**, which does not become inaccurate:
a person who accepted version 3 did, and a login that failed, failed. Both are
append-only by design. If something was recorded wrongly, record the correction
as a new event.

## What stays yours

None of this is in a library, and none of it is optional:

- **A lawful basis for each purpose**, a **privacy notice**, and your **record
  of processing** (Art. 30). Consent is only one basis; `consent` helps when you
  chose it.
- **Processors.** A data-processing agreement with your host, database, email and
  analytics vendors, and a transfer mechanism if data leaves the EEA.
- **Your own data.** Everything in your tables. Register it as a `SourceFunc`
  for the export and an `EraserFunc` for the sweeper, or the person's request is
  only partly answered.
- **Sessions keep the full IP and user agent** of the sign-in until they expire
  or are purged; the audit log's IP modes do not reach them. Keep session TTLs
  short, run `PurgeExpired`, and mention them in your notice.
- **Backups.** A deleted row survives in last night's backup. Decide how long
  backups live, and how a restore re-applies erasures (replaying the erasure
  requests since the backup is the usual answer). Destroyed audit keys must not
  live on in a backup that outlives the request.
- **Breach detection and notification** — 72 hours to the regulator (Art. 33).
  The audit log and `token.reuse` / `session.reuse` events are your evidence,
  not your process.
- **Identity checks** before answering an access or erasure request that did not
  come through a signed-in session, and answering within one month.
- **A DPIA** where your processing is high-risk, and a DPO where you are required
  to have one.
- **Encryption at rest and in transit**, database roles, and — for the audit
  tables — a role that [does not own them](/audit/storage#the-guards).

<Note>
This page is engineering guidance, not legal advice. Which of these duties
applies to you, and how, depends on your processing; have it reviewed against
your own situation.
</Note>
